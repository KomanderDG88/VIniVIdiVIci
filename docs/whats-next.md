# What's next, in full

Moved here whole from the [README](../README.md) on 26 November 2026, so that the README stays short. Nothing was left out.

In `main` since the evening of 26 November, on one session on a quiet machine (6.64 fps and a median frame of 144 ms, against 6.69 and 141 on the code before it): the original's next two commits, the fix for the leak of placed-view shapes, three fixes of the original to descriptor heaps and placed views (#1324, #1333, #1336), and some twenty switches brought from other projects, all off by default and none measured here by itself yet; the tables above say what each is. Faces and arms that turn black and come back were seen in play on 26 November; the log of a 15-minute session counts 21,001 textures changed after their upload, between which change and the next upload a shader reads the old copy, and that is the first thing being tried.

Compiled and passing the tests, and not in `main` yet: the original's exact cascade shadows on cards of 32 lanes (#1002), three more kinds of ray-only work that can be left out, with a count of how many such programs this version of the title has, the phase of every `=alt` turn inverted on request (#1341), and a trim of the layer's own memory.

What holds the measuring is memory. In the first room the process holds 16.1 to 16.8 GiB of private memory, on a machine of 32 GB, and more while the world loads: the peak of that day's eight complete sessions before the merge was between 16.5 and 19.0 GiB, and that is where the lab's guard stops a session when the rest of the machine leaves too little. The lab's runner waits for 20 GiB free before it starts one. The first of the switches that make the layer hold less was measured on the evening of 26 November, on the merged code, a quiet machine, one session each: with `VICTORY_WINDOW_KB=1024` the layer's windows over title memory came to 2,960 MB against 3,252 and private memory in the room to 16.4 GiB against 16.8, at 6.27 fps against 6.64 (median frame 155 against 144 ms), and the peak while loading did not move (18.1 against 18.2 GiB). So that switch alone is not worth its cost. In both sessions more than a gigabyte of those windows' pages was never copied. `VICTORY_WINDOW_TAILS` and title memory used in place (`VICTORY_INPLACE`) are next.

The 3D picture on Series S is poor for what look like three separate reasons, to be told apart one setting at a time on the same build:

- **Dark:** the builder of ray-tracing structures is off by default (`VICTORY_BVH_BUILDER=1` turns it on), and the title's indirect light comes from rays: whatever the sun does not reach is black. Turned on here it ended the session in an access violation in the title's code, 17 to 22 s after the builder began; with the fix to DispatchX by thread counts (fxceless/victory-fork#1292) the builder ran 13 minutes, to the end of the session, with no fault. It stays off all the same: as the layer serves it a frame takes 6 to 12 s, with the card about 5 % busy (the layer compares 10 to 20 GB of title memory a frame to find what those programs wrote, and finds nothing changed), the process reaches 23 GB of private memory, and the room looked no different in the pictures saved. A fix for that cost (fxceless/victory-fork#916) is in behind a switch and is the next thing to measure. A constant in place of that light is in as a switch (`VICTORY_AMBIENT`, from Belobug's fork). Measured in one room at 0.6, 3 and 12: the picture brightens with the level and burns out at 12, so in that room the darkness answers to that light (one room, one framing: not shown for every dark shot); a level per scene (or one that follows the title's exposure) is the open part, and a face's dark side and hair artifacts are not the light's to mend.
- **Textures:** an 8 GB card has almost no room left for the title's textures with the default reserves. With a smaller reserve for render targets 3.4 GB of textures were on the card where 2.1 GB had been, in sessions that are not otherwise comparable. With each texture's largest level left out (`VICTORY_TEXTURE_DROP_MIPS=1`, #610), which is what others with 8 GB cards report working, seven sessions of ten minutes reached the 3D scenes and ran to their time limit without a crash, and one reading showed 4.3 of 7.2 GB of the card in use; there is no paired comparison of the picture yet.
- **Edges and shimmer:** the plain stretch does not accumulate or sharpen. fxceless/victory-fork#798 makes the title use its own upscaler instead.

One pipeline the host refused is mended: the title leaves blending on for an integer render target, which the console ignores and Direct3D 12 does not allow, and the fix merged upstream (Marvisak/VIctory#1152, Pablo González) is in, ported to this tree; since then no pipeline is refused. **A correction:** an earlier version of this page put down to that pipeline the about 260 draws a frame the log counts as dropped. That was wrong. The pipeline was drawn less than once a frame; the 260 are stages of the ray-tracing structure builder, which is off (see Dark above), before the fix and after it. The person watching still saw mouths that do not move and are at times black. The probable cause is in the shader translator: the title's facial network moves data of sixteen bits that the translator took for floats where they are integers, which gives zeros on NVIDIA cards (fxceless/victory-fork#927, tippythetaidum). That fix is built on top of `main` and waits for a session that shows a face; it is not in yet.

While the player moves or a zone loads, the frame rate falls to 2 to 3 frames a second and worse: in those stretches the texture refresh waits for the title's memory to hold still while the runtime remaps it, 200 ms of each frame when the person playing moved and 2.9 s a frame during the load of a zone. `VICTORY_TEXTURE_NO_WAIT=1` reads without that wait, and now takes a read only if no mapping under it changed while it read. Measured on build `efe85c5`, one session each, over the scenes and loads before the first room (about 400 s, no input): without it 1,181 frames, the wait 242 ms a frame on average and up to 14.4 s, 46 frames of over a second holding 119 s, the longest frame 15.3 s; with it 1,242 frames, no wait, 39 frames of over a second holding 67 s, the longest 5.2 s. Smoother loads, then, and about 5 % more frames, not a higher frame rate: what is left of the long frames is not in the layer's own preparing. In the room itself, with scripted movement, 4.5 and 4.3 fps: no difference. In that session the person watching saw fewer textures: without the wait a texture only partly mapped, the usual state while it streams in, was read whole or not at all and showed nothing. That is mended since (it is read in part, as the path that waits does, under the same check) and is to be looked at again.

Then the frame rate, as things stand on 26 November. With the original's set of ten switches the frame in the first room is 147 ms, and what bounds it is the layer's own submit thread: it asks Windows about 2,000 times a frame which pages were written (39 ms), which a change that asks once for a whole reservation is to replace (built on top of `main`, waiting for a session), and it waits 20 to 40 ms for two small copies of bytes that the card writes in that same frame. `VICTORY_INDIRECT_WINDOW=1` takes the card's time in the title's lists from 91 to 53 ms and the frame to 133 ms, but it reads a stale record at times. The probable cause, read in the code and not measured yet: a window charges the list's budget for records that are never drawn, so a later draw finds the budget spent; a diagnostic that says so is written, and the mend has to keep that budget's guard against a card held busy for seconds. A build with link-time optimisation is to be measured too, as a fork made for cards of 8 GB uses one (soldsawn/victory-nvidia-8gb).

A fault of the translator is live on this card. In compute groups of two dimensions the host's waves are filled from tiles of 16 by 2 on NVIDIA, and half the threads took the other half's numbers: a probe on this RTX 4060 Ti wrote 32 of 64 words. The fix (fxceless/victory-fork#1261, Garu667) is in, ported onto this tree's paired form: 64 of 64 on the card, and 11 of the 3,243 programs translated on the way to the first room take it. With it on and off in one build that room looked the same at the moments it was looked at, so it mends a real miscalculation without a change seen there yet; faces from the front and other scenes are still to be looked at.

For a card of 8 GB. The original's memory switches as it uses them on 12 GB (`VICTORY_LEVELS_ON_DEMAND=1`, `VICTORY_TEXTURE_BACK=1`, `VICTORY_CARD_BUFFERS_MIN_KB=64`, with the limits for buffers and windows on the card at 512 MB), on top of the ten and as a set, one session: 3.3 to 3.9 fps by minutes (3.5 over the 280 s), about 3,600 draws a frame, and a head partly see-through. An observation of one session, not a measured drop: the ten had given 6.5 fps and about 4,500 draws on the build before, but the session that was to set the two side by side on the same build was cut short by the memory guard before the room. They are measured one at a time next. Two leads read in other forks are not measured here yet: the room the layer keeps on the card for buffers that are not made yet is counted as used by the rule that lets textures in (`VICTORY_CARD_ROOM_FOR_BUFFERS=0` turns that off; in the first room that is at most some 370 MB, beside the 1,536 MB our sessions keep for render targets), and textures wait meanwhile; and a texture with no room can be made of its small levels alone instead of refused (ruff-lab).

For the PC's memory. The game takes 16 to 18 GB of private memory here, and a session was cut short by our own guard when other work on the PC left less than that free. By the layer's own account in the first room: the title's own memory is the bulk of it (the console's memory is one, and the title keeps some 14.5 GB of textures in it), and the layer adds about 4.0 GB of copies of title memory, 3.3 GB of them windows of 4 MiB of which 2.1 GB of pages were ever copied. A change that stops making a buffer's whole copy for a view that has its own bytes (Marvisak/VIctory#949, Kamil Ojdana, ported onto this tree) was measured, one session each with the ten switches: private memory at its peak 17.88 GB without it and 17.60 with it, 36 windows and 150 MB fewer, the frame rate alike (6.67 and 6.57 fps). Too little for a change with no switch of its own: not merged. A smaller window (`VICTORY_WINDOW_KB`) is measured next; the staging memory that `VICTORY_STAGING_RELEASE=1` lets go was 33 MB in that room, so nothing is expected of it there.
