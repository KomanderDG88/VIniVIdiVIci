# Measurements, in full

Moved here whole from the [README](../README.md) on 26 November 2026, so that the README stays short. Nothing was left out.

## Frame rate in the world: the notes of the Status table, in full

**Latest, on the code of `main` (build `bfb2072`), median in play:** in the first room with a scripted manoeuvre of 280 s (the camera turned and the character walked every 20 s; about 4,500 draws a frame), with `VICTORY_SKIP_WC_BACK=1`, `VICTORY_COPY_PLAIN=1` and `VICTORY_TEXTURE_DROP_MIPS=1`: 1,678 frames, **5.99 fps, median frame 164 ms** (95th percentile 204 ms); and after the merge of the original (build `55160ae`, its shader cache cold) 1,663 frames, 5.94 fps and 165 ms. One session each. Not measured without those switches. **With ten more switches of the original on, as a set** (`VICTORY_ASYNC_SUBMIT=1`, `VICTORY_QUEUE2=1`, `VICTORY_JOIN_PARTS=1`, `VICTORY_BACK_OWN_VIEW=1`, `VICTORY_INDIRECT_GATHER=1`, `VICTORY_CARD_REHASH_RUNS=1`, `VICTORY_CARD_HASH_LEN=1`, `VICTORY_MERGE_GAP=64`, `VICTORY_MERGE_RUNS=4096`, `VICTORY_STAGING_POOL=0`), same build `55160ae` and manoeuvre, one session: 1,825 frames, **6.52 fps, median frame 147 ms** (95th percentile 198 ms); by minutes from the first key 6.49, 6.56, 6.46, 6.57, 6.62 and 6.54 fps, against 5.96, 5.98, 5.94, 5.89, 5.95 and 5.97 without them. A set, not a cause: what each of the ten gives is not measured, the room looked as it does without them at the moments it was looked at, and `VICTORY_MERGE_GAP` is not exact by its author's own note. **With `VICTORY_INDIRECT_WINDOW=1` added to those ten**, same build and manoeuvre, one session: 2,045 frames, **7.31 fps, median frame 133 ms** (95th percentile 210 ms); by minutes 7.37, 7.19, 7.25, 7.46, 7.53 and 7.38 fps. The card's time in the title's own lists fell from 91 to 53 ms a frame. No device was lost in this session; the original lost one in eight with this switch, so one session here does not clear it. The load before the room took longer and showed a white frame for a moment, and the person watching saw dark rectangles high on a wall of the room that they did not remember from the session without the switch: not compared under control yet, so neither claimed as a fault of the switch nor ruled out. **A fault was then found in that switch.** The original's own checker (`VICTORY_INDIRECT_WINDOW_CHECK=1`), run in a session of its own on the same build and set, compared 8.05 million records that draws read from a window with a fresh clamp of each: one differed, a record of no indices where the fresh one had 6 indices by 32 instances, so that draw drew nothing. One in millions, 476 s into that session, in the scenes before the room, and nobody has tied it to the rectangles; but the switch reads a stale record at times, and until that is mended `VICTORY_INDIRECT_WINDOW` is not recommended and the figure to go by is the one of the ten switches, 6.52 fps. What bounds the frame with all eleven on, by the layer's own account: its submit thread works 123 of the 126 ms of a usual frame, 89 of them preparing, of which 75 in copies of title memory (39 of those asking Windows, about 2,000 times a frame, which pages were written) and 20 waiting for the card, most of that wait for two copies of 2 KiB a frame that the title makes of bytes the card writes in that same frame. The card is idle for 54 ms of each frame: with this set the limit is the processor, not the card. What these are compared with, a correction to the figures published before 26 November and every earlier figure are under [Measurement history](#measurement-history). RTX 4060 Ti.

## First in-game measurement

Recorded on 25 November 2026 at commit `abf1575`, on **Windows 11 / RTX 4060 Ti / driver 617.42 / Core i7-8700K (6 cores, 12 threads) / 32 GB of RAM**, with the title's files on a 5,400 rpm hard disk. Diagnostics build, Series X profile, `VICTORY_HEAP_SYNC_TABLES=1`, frame log and card meter on.

| Run | 60-second window | Frames | Mean rate | Frame time p5 / p50 / p95 |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | ---: | :--- |
| A | First window in the world | 222 | 3.6 fps | 161 / 262 / 429 ms |
| A | About three minutes later | 188 | 3.0 fps | 115 / 303 / 672 ms |
| B, with `VICTORY_INDIRECT_WINDOW=1` added | Last minute of the run | 306 | 5.0 fps | 161 / 195 / 242 ms |

- **Reaching the world:** the first frame with 500 draws or more came about 10 minutes after the process started. The hard disk bounded the load: about 100 reads a second at 2 to 4 MB/s in the samples taken, never idle.
- **Not a controlled comparison:** the camera was not fixed. Run A's views ranged from about 530 to 2,650 draws a frame; run B stayed near 800. At about 800 draws a usual frame took 194 ms in A and 201 ms in B, so **no gain is attributed to `VICTORY_INDIRECT_WINDOW`**.
- **Where a usual frame of about 200 ms went:** the rendering thread spent about 190 ms inside submit calls, some 90 to 100 ms preparing and 60 to 100 ms waiting for the card. Copying back what shaders wrote took 86 to 141 ms for 15 to 32 MB on its own thread. The card stood idle for 93 to 134 ms of the frame, and its own work was 73 to 99 ms. On this machine the processor-side work in series bounds the frame, not the card.
- **Limits:** diagnostics counters and occasional screenshots were on, and they cost frame time. Typically 80 to 130 draws a frame were dropped with no pipeline pending, at times more. Shadows were black in the views checked. No input was tested. Both runs were stopped by the test harness's own memory guard (under 2 GiB of physical memory free), not by a crash, after about 7 and 3 minutes in the world.

### The same evening, from an SSD

The title's files were copied to an NVMe SSD on the same machine. These runs used a test build, `abf1575` plus fxceless/victory-fork#258; #258 was merged into `main` afterwards. Diagnostics build, `VICTORY_HEAP_SYNC_TABLES=1`.

| Profile | How it went | Memory |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Series X | First 3D scene of the introduction after about 4.5 minutes. Stopped at 295 s by the harness's memory guard, not by a crash. | 20.1 GiB private in the process; 201 MB of physical memory available; 43.8 GiB of commit still free |
| Series S | 800 s without a crash, closed on request. The introduction's scenes advanced. | 16.7 GiB private at most; 516 MB of physical memory available at the lowest |

- **Physical memory is the limit on this 32 GB machine**, with other programs open. The page file does not help: commit was never short.
- **Picture, Series S with #258:** dark, with incomplete geometry and faces and strong temporal artifacts. There is no matching shot without #258 to compare with, so nothing is concluded about #258 here.
- **No frame rate is reported for these runs.** An unrelated job was using about ten of the machine's twelve threads at the time, the pipeline cache was cold (about 418 ms of a 580 ms frame spent waiting for pipelines) and texture refreshes waited 89 to 342 ms a frame for the lock that mapping changes take. The log's 1.4 frames a second for the last minute measures those, not the layer.
- **Player control is still not confirmed.**

## Measurement history

The figures behind the Status table, the newest first. Each is one session unless it says otherwise.

**27 November: original textures on 8 GB.** The Series S profile, the switches of Status and `VICTORY_TABLED_ALL=1`, without `VICTORY_TEXTURE_DROP_MIPS`, with `VICTORY_RAM_TRIM=1`.

- *Held levels* (`VICTORY_HELD_LEVELS=1`, ported from edsuuu/VI-BR-PORT 8bb5142d, off by default; in testing, not in `main` yet): a texture the title places in memory gets a host copy from the first level its views show, and is remade with more levels when a view asks for one. One session of 900 s on build `4ed54e1e`: no texture slot held for want of room in any of 166 counts, where the same code without it held some 14,000 and ran at about 4.2 fps; 5.90 fps over the measured window of the scripted manoeuvre; peak game memory 17.8 GiB. The picture is not judged yet: every screenshot of that session was taken before the world appeared.
- *VI-BR-PORT's own base on this PC*, built here from its commit 8bb5142d with its files derived from XCL replaced by empty stand-ins (a local test, not part of this fork): warm-up sessions only, read from its own frame lines. About 8.6 fps in scenes of more than 3,000 draws on the Series S profile with original textures, and 2.8 on the Series X profile; peak game memory 21.7 to 22.5 GiB (Series S) and 26.9 GiB (Series X); faces missing, with hair and eyes floating. Of its frame, the card's read-back took about 61 ms, against about 16 ms here.
- *Memory.* While loading, the game reaches 17.8 GiB of private memory and some 4 GiB more in memory that is not private; with 32 GB, the lab's guard (512 MB free) stopped several sessions during the load. A guard of 320 MB until the manoeuvre starts, and 512 MB during it, is used now.

**The night of 26 to 27 November: texture room, a smaller display, and the heap pass.** On the code of `main` (build `fe2ede82`) and the ten switches of Status unless said otherwise.

- *Texture room.* Without `VICTORY_TEXTURE_DROP_MIPS=1` (each texture's largest level left out), slots the shaders pick by index were held for want of room on the card in 63 of 85 counts between 150 and 600 s, up to 18,151 of 25,000 in the opening's cafeteria; the scene came out very dark, and the room later had its bed and wall missing. With it, in 4 or 5 of some 86 counts, up to 1,004 and 4,955 in two sessions, and none in the room. So on an 8 GB card that switch is needed. By the layer's own accounts the title's render targets take 2.3 to 3.1 GB of the card when it is told the display is 3840x2160, which is the layer's default.
- *A 1920x1080 display.* With `VICTORY_DISPLAY_MODE=1920x1080` alone the picture of the world came out black: on the Series S profile the title then picks FidelityFX, whose passes are native AMD code that the layer does not translate. With it and the title's own upscaler (`VICTORY_OWN_UPSCALER=1`, in `main`; or `VICTORY_UPSCALER=6`, brought from edsuuu/VI-BR-PORT) the picture came back, but it does not leave more room for textures: over the scenes from 150 to 600 s, slots were held for room in 3 to 9 of some 80 counts a session with a peak of 2,388 to 5,747, against 4 or 5 counts and 1,004 to 4,955 at 3840x2160 (two early sessions at 1920x1080 with none were slow, cold loads). And it cost frame rate: on the code of `main`, the scripted manoeuvre, one session, 6.33 fps and a median frame of 150 ms against 6.63 and 145 ms. So the smaller display is not a gain here.
- *The heap pass.* `VICTORY_TABLED_ALL=1` (fxceless/victory-fork#1414: every slot of a table a draw binds counts as a table slot, so some 11,400 buffer views a frame are no longer left for a later pass), on build `f9f53eaa` at 1920x1080 with the title's upscaler, the scripted manoeuvre, one pair: 6.86 fps and a median frame of 143 ms against 6.37 and 151 ms; the pass over the heap 15.4 against 44.6 ms a frame, the draws the same (4,484 against 4,491), the card's time the same.
- *`VICTORY_TABLED_ALL=1` at 3840x2160* (build `f9f53eaa`, the code of `main` after the merge of 27 November), the scripted manoeuvre, one pair: 6.87 fps, median frame 142 ms, 95th percentile 169 against 6.59, 144 and 200 ms; the pass over the heap 18.4 against 41.0 ms a frame; 4,383 against 4,471 draws a frame. Without it, the merged code against the code before the merge: 6.59 against 6.62 fps.
- *Faces.* A face came out black at 346 s in one session with no slot held for room at that moment and 2,611 slots waiting in the upload queue; in every session that queue reaches 8,000 to 14,000 when the shot changes. In that session the title's own card-memory defragmenter, which moves resources in card memory, ran no dispatch until about 300 s and some 3 to 5 planning dispatches a frame from then on (counted by a diagnostics line of build `f9f53eaa`): the face came after it started. The upload queue after a change of shot, and the defragmenter, are the next suspects; `VICTORY_SKIP_DEFRAG=1` (leaves its planning out) is being tried.
- *Not gains here:* `VICTORY_BVH_NO_HIT=1` (no ray finds anything; reported elsewhere as 6.8 to 10.9 fps on the same card): 6.00 fps against 6.63, the card's time in the title's lists 95.8 against 90.7 ms a frame, and the light worse (dark backgrounds, an overbright sun in the room). `VICTORY_TEXTURE_NO_WAIT=1`: frames of a second or more while loading, 54 summing 115 s against 39 summing 79 s, because the title's own remapping took 73 s instead of 26. `VICTORY_SECTION_MB=2`: such frames summed 65 s against 79, one session whose measured window mixed a cutscene with the room.

**The evening of 26 November: the code of `main` again, a switch that draws less, and faces that turn black.** All on build `9ef09c2` (the code of `main`), the ten switches of Status, a quiet machine.

- *The same code twice:* 6.64 fps and a median frame of 144 ms at 19:36, and 6.63 fps and 144 ms (95th percentile 198) at 21:35, the scripted manoeuvre, one session each.
- *`VICTORY_CARD_BUFFERS_MIN_KB=64`* (buffers from 64 KiB kept on the card; reported elsewhere as 20.0 to 22.7 fps on an RTX 4060 Ti of 8 GB), the session after that second control: 7.77 fps and a median frame of 134 ms. But by the layer's own account it drew 3,553 draws a frame against 4,449, the preparation of a frame stayed at 84 ms, the copy back from the card fell from 5.9 to 2.5 MB a frame, and the screenshots showed the room notably darker, with heads and skin very dark. The frame is shorter because the title draws a fifth less, not because the layer does its work faster: this is not a gain, and it is not recommended. Its private memory peaked at 18.4 GiB against 16.9.
- *Faces and arms that turn black*, reported in play, were reproduced in scripted sessions with screenshots every second on the default profile: a character seen from the front in the opening's cafeteria has a black face with bright eyes in some sessions and some shots, and a clear one in others (on the default profile: black from the front, and clear from another angle and in profile, within seconds of each other in one session; clear in that shot in another session). Raising the stand-in light (`VICTORY_AMBIENT=3`) does not mend it, so it is not a lack of light. Counted with `VICTORY_BAKE_SOURCES=count`, which changes nothing: in 8 minutes the title made 204 skin-bake draws, and 44 of them sampled sources not on the card yet; with `VICTORY_BAKE_SOURCES=1` there were none such when the face came out black, in two shots in a row (later in that session 8, for want of room on the card). So the skin bake is not the cause of this case. The original's commit `e4245f3` (26 November) names black-face texture fixes: textures whose memory is filled by plain reads after their upload go back to pending. It came into `main` that night.
- *The merge of the night (build `fe2ede82`, the original's black-face texture fixes on by default),* screenshots every second, one pair of sessions of 480 s on the same binary after a warm-up: in the opening's cafeteria, two faces were drawn with their features with the new default, and one of them had none and the background was washed out with `VICTORY_PAGE_WRITES=guards`, at different moments and poses; behind the glass the face was clear with both. Frames of a second or more between 150 and 450 s: 52 (longest 6.8 s) with the default, 49 (5.7 s) with guards. In the reverse pair, the face of the woman in front came out almost all black in the cafeteria with the new default, and the guard session was stopped by the memory guard at 391 s. So these fixes are not the whole answer to black faces. The first warm-up, with a cold shader cache, was ended at 388 s by the title itself after a frame of 21.5 s whose texture refresh waited 20.6 s for 2,595 remappings by the runtime; its retry ran to the time limit.
- *The code of `main` after that merge* (build `fe2ede82`), the scripted manoeuvre, no screenshots, one session: 1,815 frames in 274.0 s, 6.62 fps, median frame 145 ms; 4,471 draws a frame against 4,449 on the code before; the copies of title memory 64.5 ms and 71.2 MB a frame against 57.2 and 64.9; the submitting thread waited 56 ms a frame for the submission lock where it had waited 59. The least free memory during the session was 417 MB, near the lab's guard.
- *Where the frame goes*, on the code before that merge and the same manoeuvre: the main submitting thread runs some 94 ms a frame inside its submissions (68 ms user, 26 ms kernel) and waits 59 ms for the submission lock, of a frame of 148 ms. With `VICTORY_CARD_BUFFERS_MIN_KB=64` that wait fell to 14 ms and the layer's wait for the card from 41 to 0 ms, while the title drew a fifth less: most of the lock's wait is the card finishing work whose bytes the title reads back in the same frame.

**What a frame is made of now, and sessions on a machine that was not quiet, 26 November.** In a session of 6.24 fps (median frame 155 ms, the ten switches of Status, the build before the dispatch spread was mended), by the layer's own account: the thread that submits is at work 155 ms of wall time a frame, of which 60 to 70 ms is its own processor time and 40 to 53 ms is waiting for the card. In the session of 6.69 fps after that fix the card ran the layer's lists about 106 ms a frame and the thread waited for it 44 ms. In the first of the two, per frame, the copies of title memory take 54 ms (71 MB), the flush of refreshes 35 ms (some 23,400 refreshes), the write-watch queries 32 ms (6.4 GB looked at, 99 % of it unchanged) and guard faults 25 ms. So less work for the card can take back at most that wait, and the thread's own work puts the ceiling near 100 ms a frame until those four come down.

The same afternoon six sessions ran with the machine busy outside the game's process. The layer's line of the machine's load gives the processors busy in all and those the process kept busy, for one second in every five; their difference over the measured window (median, in logical processors) is set here against the frame rate. It counts the system's own work as well as other programs', so it does not say whose it was: a video render of the owner's was seen at work during one of these windows, and this project's own agents were searching and merging in git during two others; for the rest it is not known. The figures: 1.3, 6.69 fps (`main`); 2.9, 6.12 (`main`, the title's own upscaler on); 3.2, 5.67 (`main`); 3.6, 5.58 (`main`, one texture switch on); 3.8, 5.30 (the next base, not in `main` yet); 4.8, 5.18 (`main`, render scale 0.5). The copies of title memory took 56, 65 and 95 ms a frame for the same megabytes at 1.3, 2.9 and 4.8. Only the first is a measurement; the others say how much a machine busy elsewhere costs and nothing about what was switched on. Free memory fell to 1.1 to 1.8 GB during three of those windows against 4.4 GB in the first, and three other sessions that afternoon were ended by our own memory guard before they reached the room, so memory is part of this too. With the render scale at 0.5 the wait for the card fell from about 43 to about 34 ms a frame, which is the one thing that session shows. `tools/compare-movement.py` now reports this figure and warns above a median of two.

**The two changes to the pass over the heap (#1043 and #1190), 26 November.** With the manoeuvre and settings of the Status table: 1,678 frames, 5.99 fps and a median frame of 164 ms on build `bfb2072`, against 1,563 frames, 5.59 fps and 175 ms (95th percentile 258 ms) on the build before the two heap changes (`63f32dd`), same settings and script, one session each, one after the other. The pass over the heap went from 68 to 34 ms a frame. **A correction:** until this pair, every session of ours also had `VICTORY_HEAP_SYNC_TABLES=1` on, a diagnostic switch that is off by default and makes that pass mirror the records draws read through tables as well. With it the same two builds gave 4.87 and 5.95 fps (the pass 132 and 53 ms), and each change alone, against an earlier base at 4.72 fps, #1043 5.70 fps and #1190 5.26 fps: most of that gain was the switch's own cost undone. The same build gave 4.87 fps with the switch and 5.59 without, so the figures of this page from before 26 November, measured with it on, are lower than the default would have given.

**Earlier figures.** Series X, yard, about 800 draws a frame: 5.0 fps measured, earlier the same day and from the hard disk. Series S, opening cinematic, about 3,000 draws: 2.9 to 3.6 fps measured (usual frames of 279 to 343 ms). Under player control: 3.2 fps measured in the minute after the report (median frame 313 ms), with an unrelated job using the processor at the time. In the interior scene with the movement prompt, nearly still, with `VICTORY_AMBIENT=0.6` and `VICTORY_TEXTURE_DROP_MIPS=1`: 3.4 fps measured over 15 s (52 frames in three batches, batch medians 287 to 297 ms), one session. In that same room, about 4,500 draws a frame, with two switches that are off by default: `VICTORY_COPY_PLAIN=alt`, turns of 20 s in one session, median frame 284 ms on against 297 ms off (3.45 against 3.28 fps; shorter on in four pairs of four); `VICTORY_SKIP_WC_BACK=1`, three sessions in the order off, on, off on build `7a262f3`, median frame 297, 261 and 296 ms; then on build `9b08008`, one session each with the same settings, 279 ms without it and 217.5 ms with it (3.5 and 4.5 fps), and 213 ms (4.6 fps) with `VICTORY_COPY_PLAIN` on as well, in turns of that same session: on top of it the plain copy adds about 1 %. The earlier three sessions had the frame-shot switch set (`VICTORY_SHOT`), which seems to cost frame time even when no shot is taken; measure without it. Snapshots of the room with and without `VICTORY_SKIP_WC_BACK` look alike, with the faults both have; they are not matched frames and no face was checked, so its effect on the picture is still open. Over the 38 minutes 44 s from the first sight of the world to the end of the long session below, with those two switches on and the character moving: 8,679 frames, 3.75 fps overall (median frame 235 ms), and by five-minute windows 4.6, 4.5, 3.5, 4.4, 4.0, 3.9, 1.8 and 3.2 fps. The 1.8 holds the load of a new zone (frames of up to 10.8 s), and the windows under 4 the stretches where the person playing moved: see What's next.

**What a frame was made of before these switches (25 November).** Then the frame rate. The largest part of a frame is the copying of title memory through the kernel, 81 to 118 ms of about 300: the copy in place and the pending synchronisation and copy-back changes (fxceless/victory-fork#665, #467, #812, #916, #857, #853) are measured one at a time, at comparable draw counts. In the room above, without those switches, the thread that submits waits about 80 ms of each 297 ms frame for the card while the thread beside it fetches card copies back, and the card has nothing of ours queued for about 150 ms. Three frames taken apart with `VICTORY_SHOT` say the same of the card's side: what the title draws took 34 ms of the card's clock in a frame of 1,700 commands and 93 ms in one of 8,400, and the layer's own passes that bring its copies of title memory up to date and compare them took several times that (frames of a capture, which weigh more than usual ones). Keeping the copies is the wall, not the drawing; of the copy-free paths that are in and off, `VICTORY_STRETCH=1` (fxceless/victory-fork#1127) covered too little of that memory here to gain anything, and `VICTORY_INPLACE=1` covers less still (12 MB of windows are in private memory at start). Measured here with `VICTORY_CENSUS=1` (#868), in windows of two to six frames of the 3D scenes: 28 to 63 MB a frame are fetched back from the card, and 93 to 95 % of them are of memory the title reserved write-combining. `VICTORY_SKIP_WC_BACK=1`, which leaves those on the card, is measured next, watching for artifacts. On this Intel machine the console-only instructions cost 8,000 to 12,000 exceptions a second in the opening cinematic, which does not bound the frame.
